Overview

Spices, millets, tea, coffee, superfoods and botanical ingredients from India's farms and processors, prepared for export.

India grows a very wide range of food crops across very different climates: pepper and cardamom in Kerala's hills, cumin and fennel in Gujarat and Rajasthan, chilli in Andhra Pradesh, tea in Assam and Darjeeling, coffee in the Western Ghats, and millets across the dry interior. Many of these products have long export histories, but buyers still need to choose processors carefully.

For food, the processor matters as much as the farm. Cleaning, sorting, sterilisation, moisture control, packing hygiene and batch testing decide whether a shipment clears your border and sells. NGB Ventures helps you find processors with the right registrations, specify residue and contaminant limits for your market, and arrange independent lab testing before goods ship.

Manufacturing capabilities

What Indian manufacturers commonly offer

01

Steam sterilisation & cleaning

Export-oriented spice processors commonly offer machine cleaning, sortex colour sorting and steam sterilisation to reduce microbial load without chemical fumigation.

02

Grinding, blending & packing

Cryogenic or conventional grinding, custom blends and seasonings, and packing in retail pouches, jars, tins or bulk bags.

03

Batch testing

Certificates of analysis for moisture, microbiology, aflatoxins, pesticide residues and heavy metals, from NABL-accredited or destination-recognised labs.

04

Certification options

Processors may hold FSSC 22000, BRCGS, ISO 22000, HACCP, organic, kosher or halal certificates. Always check scope and validity with the issuing body.

Regulatory

Compliance considerations

These are considerations to raise with your compliance advisor, testing lab or customs broker. They aren't legal advice. Regulations change, so verify current requirements for your product and market.

Indian export registrations

Food exporters typically need an FSSAI licence and an IEC. Spices exporters register with the Spices Board, tea and coffee exporters with the Tea Board and Coffee Board, and many processed foods fall under APEDA. We verify these before you order.

United States

Foreign food facilities must register with the FDA, shipments need Prior Notice, and the US importer is responsible under the FSMA Foreign Supplier Verification Program. The US ended recognition of India's NPOP organic standard in 2021, so organic claims need USDA NOP certification.

European Union & UK

Maximum residue levels apply under Regulation (EC) 396/2005. Some Indian spices and seeds have faced increased border checks, so check the current list under Implementing Regulation (EU) 2019/1793 and test accordingly.

Health claims & novel foods

Superfood and Ayurvedic products are regulated by what you claim. Health claims, supplement rules and novel-food status differ by market. Have your regulatory advisor review labels and marketing.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Can you source organic spices from India?

Yes, from processors with organic certification valid for your destination. For the US this now means USDA NOP certification. We check certificates in the certifier's database and test for residues regardless.

What is a realistic minimum order for private-label spices?

It depends on the pack format. Custom printed pouches usually carry the highest minimums because of printing set-up. Stock pouches with labels allow smaller first orders. We'll compare both.

Do you handle lab testing?

We arrange independent testing before shipment against the limits you specify, and share the reports. Testing reduces risk but can't guarantee border clearance. Your importer and regulator make that decision.

Are there export restrictions on Indian food products?

India sometimes restricts or bans exports of certain staples, such as some rice and sugar categories, to manage domestic supply. We check the current DGFT position before quoting.
