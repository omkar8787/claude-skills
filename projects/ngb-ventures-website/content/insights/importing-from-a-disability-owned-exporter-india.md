## Why supplier diversity reaches beyond borders

Many large organisations track how much they spend with businesses owned by under-represented groups, including people with disabilities. As supply chains go global, procurement teams increasingly ask whether international suppliers can be part of those programmes. India, with its depth of manufacturing and craft, is a natural place to look.

## What a disability-owned business is

Disability:IN, the organisation behind the **DOBE®** (Disability-Owned Business Enterprise) certification, sets the core test: a for-profit business that is at least 51% owned, managed and controlled by one or more people with disabilities. Certification means that ownership has been checked independently, so a buyer doesn't have to verify it alone.

## Why buying directly matters

The clearest way to count spend with a diverse supplier is to buy from that business directly. When the disability-owned company is also the **exporter of record**, shipping under its own Importer-Exporter Code (IEC), your purchase order and invoice sit with that business, not with an intermediary. That makes the spend easy to attribute and report.

- **Tier 1:** your organisation buys directly from the diverse supplier.
- **Tier 2:** you supply a large customer, and your purchases from a diverse supplier support that customer's diverse-spend reporting.

## What certification will and won't do

Once granted, certification can make a supplier easier to find through Disability:IN's supplier directory, and it gives procurement teams verified ownership to rely on. It doesn't guarantee contracts, preference or pricing. Each company's programme sets its own rules, and some only recognise certain supplier locations or certifying bodies.

## Questions to ask before you buy

- Is the certification granted, or is the application still in progress? Ask to see the current status.
- Is the supplier the exporter of record on your shipments?
- How does your own programme treat an India-based diverse supplier?
- Are the usual safeguards in place: an NDA, verified makers, independent inspection and clear contracts?
