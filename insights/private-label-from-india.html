Private label from Indiafrom first sample to shelf

A practical path for brands launching private-label products made in India: choosing the product, finding the maker, developing samples, packaging, compliance and the first order.

Private label, white label, OEM

White label usually means a maker's existing product with your label on it. It's fast, but others can sell the same thing. Private label means a product developed or adapted for you, so it's more distinctive. OEM, or build-to-print, means the factory makes exactly to your design and specification. Most new brands start with white label or light customisation, then move towards private label as volumes grow.

Pick a product India is good at

You'll get better quality and pricing where India has deep clusters: cotton textiles, home décor, brass and metalware, wood, leather, natural personal care, spices and tea, jute and paper packaging, and engineered components. Match the product to the cluster before you match it to a factory.

Develop the sample like a product manager

Write the specification, share references and agree in writing what 'approved' means. Expect two or three sample rounds. Finish with a sealed golden sample that both sides sign, because it becomes the standard for production and inspection.

Packaging is half the product

Packaging carries your brand, protects the goods and has to meet labelling rules in your market. Design it for the channel, whether that's a retail shelf, a marketplace listing or a gift box. Remember that printed packaging has its own lead time and minimum order.

Compliance before you scale

Each category has its own rules: food-contact testing for kitchenware, chemical limits for textiles, cosmetic regulations for personal care, and safety standards for toys. Test the golden sample in an accredited lab for your market before the first production order, not after.

The first order

Keep the first order small enough to manage risk, but large enough to meet the factory's minimum and give you sales data. Inspect before shipment, and plan freight and duties into your landed cost. Then build your brand story and listings while the goods are at sea.
