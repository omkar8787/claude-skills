Incoterms for India importsEXW, FOB, CIF, DAP and DDP explained

Which Incoterms 2020 rules buyers commonly use when importing from India, who pays what, where risk transfers, and practical pitfalls.

Why the Incoterm matters

Incoterms® 2020 rules, published by the International Chamber of Commerce, define which party pays for which costs and where risk passes from seller to buyer. They don't set the price, transfer ownership or replace a sales contract. Always state the rule, the named place and the version, e.g. 'FOB Nhava Sheva, Incoterms® 2020'.

EXW (Ex Works)

The seller makes goods available at its premises, and the buyer handles everything else, including export clearance in India. Export clearance by a foreign buyer can be impractical, so EXW is often a poor fit for international shipments. FCA is usually the better choice.

FCA (Free Carrier)

The seller delivers the goods, cleared for export, to the buyer's carrier at a named place. It works well for containerised shipments and for buyers who appoint their own forwarder.

FOB (Free On Board)

The seller clears goods for export and loads them on board the vessel at the named port. Risk transfers once the goods are on board. FOB is widely used for sea freight from Indian ports, although the ICC notes FCA is often more appropriate for containers.

CIF / CIP

The seller pays freight (and minimum insurance under CIF) to the destination port, but risk transfers at origin, when goods are loaded. Buyers should check the insurance cover and remember they bear the transit risk.

DAP / DDP

Under DAP the seller delivers to a named destination, ready for unloading, and the buyer handles import clearance and duties. Under DDP the seller also pays import duties and taxes, which requires the seller to act as, or arrange, the importer of record. DDP from India needs careful structuring.

Practical recommendations

Choose an Incoterm based on who controls freight and who has the expertise to handle each step. Buyers with an established forwarder often prefer FCA or FOB. First-time importers sometimes prefer CIF or DAP, but should understand the trade-off in control and transparency.

  • Always name a precise place or port
  • Align the Incoterm with your payment terms and insurance
  • Make sure your landed-cost model uses the same basis
