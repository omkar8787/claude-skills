MilletsIndia's ancient grains, ready for global shelves

Ragi, jowar, bajra and the small millets: what they are, where India grows them, which products buyers can source, and what to check for export.

Grains that grew before rice and wheat took over

Long before the Green Revolution made rice and wheat India's staples, millets fed much of the subcontinent. Finger millet (ragi), sorghum (jowar) and pearl millet (bajra) grow where water is scarce and soils are poor. They still anchor farming across the dry interior, from Rajasthan and Maharashtra to Karnataka.

The United Nations declared 2023 the International Year of Millets, at India's proposal. That brought new attention, new processing capacity and a wave of millet products aimed at health-conscious consumers worldwide.

Meet the main millets

Each millet has its own character and its own best uses. Buyers usually start with one of these:

  • Ragi (finger millet): earthy and naturally rich in calcium. Used in flours, porridges, cookies and malted drinks.
  • Jowar (sorghum): mild and versatile. Used in flatbread flours, popped snacks and gluten-free baking blends.
  • Bajra (pearl millet): nutty and hearty. Used in flatbreads and multigrain blends.
  • Small millets (foxtail, little, kodo, barnyard, proso): quick-cooking grains for rice replacements and ready-to-cook mixes.

What you can actually source

Indian processors offer millets at every stage, from cleaned whole grain to finished retail products. The more processed the product, the more important the processor's food-safety system becomes.

  • Cleaned and graded whole grains in bulk
  • Flours and multigrain atta blends
  • Flakes, puffs and popped millet
  • Ready-to-cook mixes: dosa, upma, khichdi, pancake
  • Snacks, cookies and breakfast cereals
  • Private-label retail packs

Quality points that matter

Millets are hardy crops, but the processing is where quality is won or lost. Ask for the specification first, then test against it.

  • Moisture and storage: high moisture invites insects and mould during sea transit
  • Foreign matter and stones: ask about destoning and colour-sorting
  • Mycotoxins and pesticide residues: test against your market's limits
  • Gluten-free claims: oats and wheat are processed in some of the same facilities, so cross-contact controls and testing matter
  • Shelf life of flours: whole-grain flours can turn rancid, so agree packing and best-before dates

Export paperwork in brief

Food exporters in India typically need an FSSAI licence and an Importer-Exporter Code, and many grain and processed-food products fall under APEDA registration. On the destination side, the US requires FDA food facility registration and Prior Notice for each shipment, and the EU applies maximum residue levels and labelling rules. Organic claims need certification that is valid in your market.

How to start

Begin with one hero product, such as a ragi flour, a millet cookie or a ready-to-cook mix, and define the specification and pack format. Ask for samples from two or three processors, and test those samples in a lab before you commit. We can help with the shortlist, the specification and the testing.
